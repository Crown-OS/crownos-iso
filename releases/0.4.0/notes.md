## Highlights

- **Updates bring new defaults.** CrownOS keeps its default settings in `/usr/share/crownos/defaults`, and each update replaces them. A setting that you changed always stays yours. Your files in `~/.config/crownos` show the defaults as comments, so you can see what you can change.
- **New wallpapers.** Nine new wallpapers, and Amber Eclipse is the new default. If your wallpaper was one of the removed ones, the update changes it to Amber Eclipse.
- **The full desktop on installed systems.** The bar, the dock, notifications and the clipboard start with each session.
- **Privacy settings.** Settings has a new Privacy group with Screen Context and App Access pages. The Keybinds page shows and edits app shortcuts.
- **Packages from the internet.** The installer downloads the CrownOS packages that the ISO does not carry, during the install or after the first start.
- **Remote Desktop.** CrownOS Remote Desktop is now part of the system.
- **Better laptop displays.** CrownOS scales a laptop panel by its size.
- **Fira Code** is the monospace font, and Kitty has translucent defaults.
- **Security.** Only a parent process can trace another process (`kernel.yama.ptrace_scope = 1`). Consent dialogs open one at a time and cannot be answered while hidden.

The update restarts the computer.
